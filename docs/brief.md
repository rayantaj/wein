# وين؟ (Wein?): the brief

Refined 23 Oct 2026, by hq overnight. The idea came from a "surprise me" brief: something fun that can go live on GitHub Pages in one night.

## The idea

A daily game, like Wordle for Saudi geography. Every day there's one hidden Saudi city. You guess a city; the game says how far away the hidden one is, in which direction, and how close you are as a percentage. Six guesses. When you're done, you share a grid of coloured squares and arrows that gives nothing away. The same city for everyone, changing at midnight in Riyadh.

Nobody has built this for Saudi Arabia yet: the global games (Worldle, Closest City Game) use the whole world, which makes most Saudi cities a guess in the dark. A Saudi-only list of about a hundred cities is the gap, and it's what makes the game fair and fun for someone who grew up here.

## Who it's for

Saudis and people living in Saudi Arabia who play Wordle-style games on their phones, and share the result in WhatsApp groups. Arabic first; an English switch for expats and for sharing abroad.

## The first version: what a person can do

1. **Open the page and play today's puzzle:** type a city name (Arabic or English) and pick it from the list; the game shows distance in km, a direction arrow and a closeness percentage for each guess, up to six.
2. **Win or lose and see the answer:** on a win, "You got it in n"; on a loss, the hidden city's name. Either way, the result stays on the page until tomorrow.
3. **Share the result:** one tap copies a text like `وين؟ #12 4/6` with a row of squares and arrows per guess, and the link.
4. **Get a hint when stuck:** from the third guess on, the hidden city's region is shown.
5. **See streaks and stats:** played, won, current and best streak, and a bar per number of guesses. Kept on the phone, no account.
6. **Keep playing:** after today's puzzle, a practice mode with a random city, as many rounds as you like.
7. **Switch language:** Arabic (right-to-left) or English, remembered.

## What's out

- No accounts, no server, no leaderboard: everything lives in the browser.
- No photos, satellite images or trivia about the cities.
- No ads, no payment.
- No native app: it's a web page that works on a phone, installable to the home screen.

## What done looks like

A public page at `https://rayantaj.github.io/wein/` that Ray opens on his phone in the morning, plays, and shares in a WhatsApp group without embarrassment: the cities are real and placed right, the Arabic reads naturally, the game is fast, and it works offline after the first visit.

## Language

Arabic first, English second. The interface, the city names and the share text exist in both.
